Astrology: a way back to yourself.

There is a whole universe above us. What if studying it could help us better understand the one within us?

A little history

For thousands of years, people have looked to the sky for meaning, guidance, and a deeper understanding of their place in the world. Astrology grew from the belief that life on Earth is connected to the movements and cycles of the cosmos. Across cultures and throughout history, astrologers have studied celestial patterns to interpret significant events, recognize cycles, and explore the relationship between the individual and the world around them.

Today, astrology continues to offer a symbolic language for self-discovery. Not as a set of rules to live by, but as a way to ask better questions, recognize our patterns, and make more intentional choices.

What Is a Natal Chart?

Think of your natal chart as a portrait of the sky at the exact moment you were born.

Using your birth date, time, and location, a natal chart maps the positions of the Sun, Moon, planets, and other significant points in the sky. It is a personal celestial snapshot, capturing a moment that becomes the starting point for your astrological story.

The chart is divided by the horizon into two halves. The upper half represents the sky above the horizon at your birthplace, while the lower half represents what was beneath it. Whether you were born during the day or at night also matters, influencing how astrologers traditionally interpret the roles and relationships of the planets.

Each planet represents a different part of the human experience. The signs describe how those themes may express themselves, the houses show where they may unfold in life, and the relationships between planets add another layer of complexity.
